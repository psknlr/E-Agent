Title: Dataset for Iterative and data-driven ortholog mining enables reliable discovery of stereoselective ketoreductases.
DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.19574983
URL: https:/10.5281/zenodo.19574983


Overall content:

Experimental data underlying main-text figures & tables.
Experimental data underlying Supporting Information figures & tables.

Data generation:
The data was generated by preparing crude cell extracts from enzymatically lysed bacterial cell pellets (for substrates 2a–5a) or clarified lysates via centrifugation (for 1a), then conducting biocatalytic reductions in 96-well format: crude extracts were combined with a cofactor-regenerating mix (NADH/NADPH, glucose, GDH) and substrates (10 mM final) for 20 h incubation at 20 °C, while clarified lysates were used in a continuous UV spectrophotometric assay monitoring substrate depletion at 340 nm.
Samples were subsequently quenched (methanol or ethyl acetate), processed by centrifugation/extraction, and analyzed by chiral HPLC-UV (for 2a and 1a diastereoselectivity) or GC-FID (for 3a–5a) using calibration curves to quantify conversion, enantiomeric excess (>99% ee when the opposite enantiomer was undetectable), and diastereomeric excess.
Melting temperatures of wildtype enzymes were determined by differential scanning fluorimetry using a Prometheus Panta instrument.
Enzymes expressed in 96-deep-well plates had cell pellets lysed by three freeze-thaw cycles, clarified lysates prepared in 10 mM potassium phosphate buffer pH 7.0 via agitation and centrifugation, and supernatants heated from 20 to 95 °C (1 °C min⁻¹ ramp) in capillaries, with Tm values extracted using PR.Panta Analysis software (v1.6.3).

Notes on data processing:

GC/LC peak areas for different substrates and products and retention times are provided together with calibration curves (and where applicable averages and SD).
When authentic product standards were available, response factors were derived from external calibrations and used to correct detector-response differences.
For these cases, conversions were calculated as: Conversion (%) = P_final / (P_final + S_final) × 100.

All experimental datasets are means of at least three independent replicates (n=3) with SD where applicable.
